# 16 — Android TV

The TV app, `:tv`: an Android TV and Google TV app under the phone app's application id, in the same `generic` and `gplay` flavours, built on Compose for TV's Material 3 (`androidx.tv:tv-material`) and the phone's repositories in `:core:data`, never the phone's UI modules. It asks for `android.software.leanback` and does not require a touchscreen, so Play offers it to TVs only, and it shows in the TV's app row by its banner: the app's mark and name on sand. Everything works with the D-pad alone, and a mouse, a pointer remote or a touch clicks too: Compose for TV's buttons and cards answer the D-pad only, so each also takes a tap (`Modifier.tap`). There is no composer.

## Signing in

A TV has no browser for a sign-in page to come back from, so it signs in out of band (`urn:ietf:wg:oauth:2.0:oob`, `OAuthIdentity.OUT_OF_BAND_REDIRECT`), which Nextcloud Social and Mastodon both serve:

1. The person enters the server with the TV's keyboard or voice input.
2. The TV registers with the out-of-band redirect and shows the server's sign-in page as a QR code (the shared `QrCode` from `:core:designsystem`). The page's address is around 350 characters with its PKCE challenge, too long to type, so the TV does not show it as text; the server has no short form of it.
3. The person scans it with a phone, signs in there and allows Aloha Social; the server's page then shows a code.
4. The person types the code on the TV, and `SignInCoordinator.completeWithCode` exchanges it with PKCE, which stands in for the state a callback would carry.

A code tried is used up, as a callback is: a refused one starts over with a new page to scan and says why ("That code didn't work"). A sign-in started before the process stopped comes back with its page. Nextcloud Social's codes are 60 characters, which is a lot to type with a remote; the TV's voice input helps.

The QR code sits at the top right, because Google TV's keyboard opens over the lower middle of the screen as soon as the code field has focus. The account is kept as the phone keeps its accounts, in the Keystore-encrypted vault, with the TV's own token; Account shows who the TV is signed in as and signs it out, giving the token back to the server. Nothing on the TV is backed up or moved to a new device.

## Screens

Navigation 3 destinations; Back goes back.

| Screen | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Home | Shelves, each a row the D-pad moves along: Continue watching, From people you follow, Shorts, Channels you follow, From everyone, Trending. The video and shorts shelves are the phone's Video and Shorts timelines, narrowed by the server where it can (`only_video`, `only_short`) and by each post's kind where it cannot, and page as the reader nears their end. Search and Account at the top. |
| Watch | The video full screen on Media3's own controls, which take the D-pad: centre plays and pauses, left and right seek along the time bar. It starts where the reader left off, falls back a rung when a source fails (the phone's `LadderPlayback`), and tells the server where the reader got to every ten seconds while playing, at once on a pause and when the page goes. Down leads to Follow, Report, the chapters to jump to, what the video says and its comments to read. |
| Shorts | One short per screen, up and down to move, centre to pause; one player with the next shorts loaded ahead (the phone's `ShortsPlayback`). Opened from the Shorts shelf at the short chosen; a short on another shelf opens on the watch page. |
| Channel | Someone's media posts as a grid, from Channels you follow or search. |
| Search | Videos and shorts, and people as channels, from the reader's server. |
| Account | Who the TV is signed in as, and Sign out. |

A card that keeps focus for 1.2 seconds plays a muted preview in place of its still, and focus comes back to the card it left when the reader returns from a video.

## Safety

Sensitive media follows the reader's own policy, `reading:expand:media`, as on the phone: unless it shows all, a card marked sensitive shows no still and plays no preview, and a sensitive video or short waits behind its warning until the reader asks for it. Report on the watch page sends the video to the reader's server's moderators as spam, illegal or something else, the reasons that ask nothing more of a reader holding a remote; a report for broken rules, which asks which, is the phone's.

## Tested, and not yet

- Unit tests: the sign-in by code against the mock server (the QR page with the out-of-band redirect, the code signing in, a refused code starting over); the shelves from a server's timelines (videos and shorts of those followed, people, everyone's, trending, a sensitive video covered); the watch page (a sensitive video waiting, chapters and comments, following, a report naming the video and its reason). `SignInAcceptanceTest` checks the out-of-band exchange against every mock server.
- Screenshots at 1080p, on the Accessibility Test Framework's checks: sign-in in each step, the shelves (also empty and at 200 % font), a covered and a failed video, a covered short, search and the account.
- D-pad: every card on every shelf and the top row, the watch page's follow and report and the report's reasons, the shorts pager, the sign-in's field and buttons, without touch. Leaving the player's own view downwards needs the view system, which a JVM test does not run.
- On a Google TV emulator (Android 16) against the dev instance: signing in by code, the shelves, the preview, focus coming back, playback with the controls, leaving the player for the page under it, a report sent, a sensitive video behind its warning.
- Not yet: on a real TV or a Chromecast with Google TV.
